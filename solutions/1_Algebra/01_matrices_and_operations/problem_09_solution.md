### Exercise 9. Composing Transformations
Let

$$
S=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 1 \\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad R=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -1 \\
1 & 0 \\
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The matrix $S$ describes nonuniform scaling, while $R$ describes a $90^\circ$ counterclockwise rotation.
For

$$
x=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
2 \\
\end{pmatrix}
$$

compute $RSx$ and $SRx$. Compare the results and explain geometrically why the order of these transformations matters.

> **Why this exercise:** interprets matrix multiplication as composition of transformations.

# Exercise 9 Solution

Let the matrices $S$ (nonuniform scaling) and $R$ ($90^\circ$ counterclockwise rotation), along with the vector $x$, be defined as follows:

$$
S = \begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 
R = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & -1 \\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad 
x = \begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

---

### 1. Compute $RSx$ and $SRx$

#### Compute $RSx$
- **Result:**
  $$
  RSx = \begin{pmatrix}
  -2 \\
  2
  \end{pmatrix}
  $$
- **Main Steps:**
  1. First, apply $S$ to vector $x$: 
     $$
     Sx = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2(1) + 0(2) \\ 0(1) + 1(2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
     $$
  2. Next, apply $R$ to the resulting vector $Sx$: 
     $$
     RSx = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0(2) + (-1)(2) \\ 1(2) + 0(2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
     $$

#### Compute $SRx$
- **Result:**
  $$
  SRx = \begin{pmatrix}
  -4 \\
  1
  \end{pmatrix}
  $$
- **Main Steps:**
  1. First, apply $R$ to vector $x$: 
     $$
     Rx = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0(1) + (-1)(2) \\ 1(1) + 0(2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
     $$
  2. Next, apply $S$ to the resulting vector $Rx$: 
     $$
     SRx = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2(-2) + 0(1) \\ 0(-2) + 1(1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
     $$

---

### 2. Comparison and Geometric Explanation

- **Comparison:** 
  Comparing the final vectors, $RSx = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $SRx = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Clearly, $RSx \neq SRx$, which demonstrates that matrix multiplication (and the composition of linear transformations) is noncommutative.

- **Geometric Explanation:**
  - **In $RSx$ (Scaling first, then Rotation):** The initial vector $x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is stretched horizontally by a factor of 2, moving it to $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. When this stretched vector is subsequently rotated $90^\circ$ counterclockwise, its coordinates swap and change signs, landing at $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
  - **In $SRx$ (Rotation first, then Scaling):** The vector $x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is first rotated $90^\circ$ counterclockwise, landing at $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. When this rotated vector is then scaled horizontally by a factor of 2, its $x$-coordinate is doubled (from $-2$ to $-4$), resulting in $\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

  Because the second transformation operates on the *intermediate* position generated by the first transformation, changing the order changes the final coordinates. This geometric reality underlies the algebraic noncommutativity of matrix multiplication.
